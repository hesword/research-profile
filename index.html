About

I am Professor of Mobile Networks and AI at the University of Essex. My research focuses on wireless and mobile networking, 5G/6G systems, connected and autonomous vehicles, edge computing and intelligence, artificial intelligence, computer vision, and intelligent document processing. A recurring theme is the use of communication, computing and AI to make connected systems safer, more efficient and more capable of cooperation.

My work spans fundamental research, international collaborative projects, technology transfer and industrial deployment. I have published more than 200 peer-reviewed papers and have worked with academic and industrial partners in the UK, Europe and internationally.

Research Areas

Connected and autonomous vehicles

Cooperative perception, V2X communication, edge-assisted autonomous driving, coordinated decision-making, driving safety and intelligent transport systems.

Semantic communication and edge intelligence

Task- and semantic-aware vehicular communication, semantic vehicular edge computing, multimodal information sharing, resource allocation and distributed intelligence for communication-constrained systems.

AI for autonomous driving

Object detection, task-oriented visual perception, digital-twin and scene-reconstruction methods, driving knowledge assessment, large language models and knowledge-enhanced decision support.

Document intelligence and workflow automation

Scene-text detection, OCR, key-information extraction, document structuring, knowledge graphs and the automation of financial, taxation and logistics workflows.

Innovation & Impact

Document intelligence and workflow automation

Research on object detection and scene-text recognition was translated into practical document-intelligence technology. A key approach treats semantically important text fields as visual objects that can be directly detected, localised and recognised, supporting key-information extraction and structured document processing. The technology was developed for Onlyou and extended from OCR to workflow automation for financial and taxation applications.

  • Cloud and local deployment through Onlyou services and the Lingyao receipt/document-recognition system.
  • Operational records report processing of up to 50,000 receipts per day at over 98.5% accuracy.
  • Confirmed deployment/customer examples include the Agricultural Bank of China and Shanghai Customs District.
  • Five granted patents cover text recognition, document structuring, OCR workflow optimisation and knowledge-graph technologies.

SROIE benchmark

I co-organised the ICDAR 2019 Competition on Scanned Receipt OCR and Information Extraction (SROIE). The competition introduced a public dataset of 1,000 scanned receipt images and benchmarked text localisation, OCR and key-information extraction. The benchmark has since been widely reused in document-intelligence research and open-source frameworks.

Continuing industrial application

The later UKRI/Innovate UK Knowledge Transfer Partnership with IFE Global Logistics extends document intelligence into an LLM-enabled transport-management workflow, including automated extraction of logistics information, identification of shipment origins and destinations, and prediction of vessel arrival and journey times.
